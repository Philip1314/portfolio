Think in systems

How I approach a messy problem.

The strongest work is not just a finished answer. It is a clear method: understand the issue, protect the facts, make a decision, and leave the next person with useful context. These are illustrative case studies, not claims of measured client results.

Data quality · Demonstration01 / 04

When a spreadsheet cannot be trusted yet

Before a report is useful, the source data needs consistent formats, transparent assumptions, and a way to catch suspicious values.

Google SheetsXLOOKUPData validation
View approach

Normalize text, define a duplicate-selection rule, preserve missing values as missing, validate dates and amounts, and flag uncertain rows for manual review. Compare before-and-after samples and document assumptions before reporting accuracy.

Email support · Sample scenario02 / 04

When a support issue needs a handoff

A good escalation does more than pass a ticket along. It gives the next person enough verified context to act without starting over.

Written communicationCase notesEscalation
View approach

Acknowledge the operational impact, explain next steps without promising an unconfirmed resolution time, and request only the information needed. The internal handoff records symptoms, checks completed, timestamps, error details, impact, and requested action.

Process design · Concept03 / 04

When a process has too many dead ends

Move the right decision to the beginning of the workflow so people do not spend time on steps that cannot lead to a valid outcome.

SOP redesignDecision treeProcess clarity
View approach

Verify eligibility first; if eligible, continue to troubleshooting; if not, explain the applicable policy and alternative. Measure any time savings against a defined baseline before calling it an achieved result.

Customer support · Sample scenario04 / 04

When instructions need to work the first time

Turn a vague explanation into a sequence a customer can follow, with clear checkpoints and no promises that have not been verified.

Customer educationKnowledge baseClarity
View approach

Confirm what is known, state the current status, provide numbered steps, avoid claiming changes were completed unless verified, and give a clear route to further assistance.

Accuracy note: Label actual workplace contributions as professional experience only when they genuinely occurred. Add metrics only when the baseline, method, sample size, and period can be explained.

AI & data quality

Judgment matters as much as the label.

AI evaluation is not about choosing the answer that sounds best. It is about applying the task rubric, checking the evidence, and explaining the decision consistently. I bring a detail-focused approach to guideline-based rating and annotation tasks.

✓

LLM output evaluation

Review responses against explicit criteria such as factual support, instruction-following, relevance, safety, and consistency.

  • Separate factual errors from style preferences
  • Use rubric criteria consistently
  • Record concise evidence for a rating
◉

Audio & language tasks

Experience and practice with audio-related tasks, transcription, and bilingual English/Filipino content where applicable.

  • Follow task-specific transcription rules
  • Review text against audio evidence
  • Maintain consistency in formatting
▧

Annotation & relevance

Guideline-based review of visual content, search relevance, and structured labeling tasks when assigned.

  • Apply the rubric rather than assumptions
  • Flag ambiguous cases appropriately
  • Protect confidential project materials
01 — EVIDENCE

What can I verify?

Ground judgments in the prompt, source material, and task criteria instead of assumptions or personal preference.

02 — CONSISTENCY

Would I rate it the same way again?

Apply the same rubric across examples and distinguish a genuine error from a stylistic difference.

03 — CLARITY

Can someone follow my reasoning?

Keep justifications concise, specific, and tied to the criteria that support the decision.

Portfolio integrity: Public examples should use original or authorized material only. Never share confidential task instructions, client data, or restricted screenshots.
